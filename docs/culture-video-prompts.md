# Culture lesson video prompts - Veo 3

Two clips for the Week 1 culture lessons. Anime, no dialogue, ambient audio only, because you
narrate live and a classroom clip that depends on its own audio is a clip that fails.

Veo 3 gives you roughly 8 seconds. That is ONE beat, not a story - both prompts below are built
around a single change of understanding, which is the only thing that reads at that length.

---

## 1. The Mind Gym - lesson C1.03

For "this room is a gym for your mind - we come here to get stronger, not to prove we arrived
strong." The beat is the second attempt, not the success.

```
Modern 2D anime, television-series quality. Crisp linework, soft cel shading, painterly
background, warm late-afternoon light falling through high windows in long dusty shafts.

A school weight room. A skinny twelve-year-old boy in an oversized gym shirt sits at a bench,
both hands on an empty barbell. Behind him, softly out of focus, two older and visibly stronger
teenagers are mid-workout, absorbed in their own sets - they never look over, and nobody is
mocking anyone. The boy lifts the bar. It wobbles and tilts. He lowers it back to the rack. He
holds still for a moment, exhales, re-sets his grip wider, and lifts again - steadier this time,
still not easy.

Camera: slow push in from a low three-quarter angle, ending close on his hands on the bar.
Handheld micro-movement, shallow depth of field.

Audio: weight-room ambience only - the distant clink of plates, an industrial fan, the muffled
squeak of shoes on rubber flooring. No dialogue, no voiceover, no music.

Do not include: any text or on-screen words, logos, smiling at the camera, thumbs up, flexing,
a crowd, cheering, slow motion, a triumphant lighting change, or a final hero shot.
```

**Why it works on 11-year-olds:** the older kids ignore him. The moment a video puts an audience
around the struggling kid it becomes a story about being watched, which is the opposite of the
point. The clip ends mid-rep on purpose - nobody gets ripped in a day.

## 2. Lost on the way to a friend's house - lesson C2.04

For the Help Contract: "I don't know" versus "I took Street A, then turned right at Street B."
The beat is the boy realizing the information was above his head the whole time.

```
Modern 2D anime, television-series quality. Crisp linework, soft cel shading, painterly
background. Dusk in a quiet residential neighborhood - deep blue sky, warm orange windows,
long shadows.

A twelve-year-old girl stands alone at a four-way intersection with a phone held to her ear.
She turns a slow half-circle, scanning - every house on every corner looks the same. Her
shoulders drop and she gives a small helpless shrug, still holding the phone. Then something
catches her eye above her. The camera cranes slowly upward to reveal a street sign on a pole
directly over her head, and past it a corner store with a lit sign and a red mailbox on the
curb. Her expression shifts - not delight, just recognition - and she turns toward the sign,
lifting her free hand to point at it while she keeps talking.

Camera: starts close and slightly handheld on the girl, then a smooth slow crane up to the
street sign and the storefront behind it.

Audio: quiet evening street ambience only - crickets, one distant passing car, a faint
unintelligible murmur from the phone speaker. No dialogue, no voiceover, no music.

Do not include: readable subtitles or captions, logos, brand names, a friend arriving, a car
pulling up, a map or GPS interface, a happy ending, or anyone smiling at the camera.
```

**One honest limitation:** Veo garbles small lettering, so the street sign will read as a street
sign by shape and placement but its actual words will likely be nonsense. That is fine here - the
whole point is that a landmark exists, not what it says - but do not plan on generating a legible
"STREET A." If you need the names, add them yourself afterward.

## Getting a usable take

- **Generate four, keep one.** These models miss more than they hit. Judge on whether the single
  beat is legible with the sound off, since that is how the room will see it.
- **Reuse the seed** of a take you almost liked and change one clause. Rewriting the whole prompt
  restarts the lottery.
- **If a face goes uncanny**, add `filmed from behind and in three-quarter profile, face never
  fully to camera` - it fixes it more reliably than asking for a better face.
- **If it feels corny**, the cause is almost always an added emotional beat the prompt did not
  ask for: a smile, a swell of light, a friend appearing. Push the "do not include" list harder
  rather than rewriting the scene.
- **Loop it silent** on the projector while you talk. Both clips are written to survive being
  watched three times in a row.
