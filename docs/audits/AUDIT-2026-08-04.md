## 1. Verdict

Deployable tomorrow, but only because the teacher already knows the workarounds - and one defect makes every other line in this report conditional: **the site has not deployed in 22 commits, so the code in `main` and the code in the classroom are different products.** The single biggest risk is not any individual bug; it is that fixes for known classroom-stopping bugs are sitting in `main`, believed shipped, while the projectors run the version that still has them - I confirmed one such fix (`88a0a84`, "the slide overlay was covering pace") is committed, correct, and absent from the running site. The lesson engine itself is genuinely good: I ran a real published lesson across control panel, both projectors and a joined student device, and the authored content, the state strip, the discussion beat timeline and the embedded manipulative all reached the room correctly. What is weakest is not the plumbing but the reading distance - the largest text a student must read on the main projector measures 27.9px, roughly 40% of what this project's own arithmetic says the back of the room needs - and the evidence spine, which still does not connect the day's most important assessment to any mastery bar.

## 10. Pedagogical assessment

The lesson I ran is better mathematics teaching than most software will let you author, and the software is at its best exactly where it stops trying to be a slide deck.

Take the CRA arc in M1.T1.L2-D1 seriously for a moment. `concrete` is *"Build every rectangle you could make from 24 tiles"* - a genuinely physical act with a physical constraint, not an abstract problem wearing a picture. `representational` is *"The Two Columns... what do you notice?"* - the rectangles become an ordered record, which is a real bridge and not a second abstract rep. `abstract` is *"Put your list in order. 1 2 3 4 6 8 12 24. Which two combine to 24?"* - the arch notation arrives only after the student has something to arch. That is a defensible C-to-R-to-A progression, and the fact that the whole thing is 13 minutes of a 48-minute day means abstract does **not** arrive too fast. Better still, the inside-phase gradual release the brief asks about is genuinely authored, not implied: the tool phase is two consecutive steps, *"7. The Tool, Together"* then *"8. Your Own Reps"* - demo, then reps, with different clocks and different strip settings (VOICE 0 silent for the demo). That is the release living inside the phase, which is the thing most curriculum software gets wrong by labelling a slide "We Do" and moving on.

So the pedagogy is real. Three things undercut it.

**The screen words fight the tasks.** `universalStateTitle()` renders **I DO** for `concrete`, and in this lesson `concrete` is a partner build with VOICE set to "1 partner" and SUPPLIES "In your hands." The projector tells thirty 6th graders *I do* while the state strip beside it tells them to talk to a partner and hold manipulatives. A student who has been taught that I Do means *watch* is now getting two contradictory instructions from the same screen. The CRA vocabulary and the gradual-release vocabulary are different axes - a concrete phase can be teacher-modelled or student-explored - and collapsing them into one label makes the label lie whenever the lesson is well designed. The state bank offering *both* vocabularies as separate pickable states (**B18**) is the same confusion surfacing in the authoring tool.

**The evidence spine still does not close.** This is the deepest issue and it is not new. Seven tools emit `reportToolResult`; the other sixteen emit nothing. Assignment attempts land in `practice_assignment_attempts` and never reach `responses`. Poll answers - which is what a Fist-to-Five, a learning check and an exit ticket all become - land in `poll_answers` and never reach `responses` either, and `recompute` reads only `iready_scores`, `responses` and `checkpoint_results`. So on the day I ran, the two readiness checks and the fist-to-five would have produced live signal for the teacher's next move and **moved no mastery bar at all**. For a system whose stated priority after the ink surface is data collection, the day's designed assessment is a dead end. And `formative.sql`'s own header comment claims the opposite. Misleading copy about what is being measured is worse than no copy, because it stops anyone from looking.

**Reading distance is treated as a style question when it is an access question.** 27.9px for the text a student must read, on a screen that is 25 feet from the back row, against this project's own published 85px arithmetic. The largest thing on the main projector is the word "Launch." A student two years below grade level - exactly the student the CRA arc is built for - gets the least from a screen they cannot resolve. The `/weekly-display` board already solved this properly with a fixed 1920x1080 stage and an 88px floor; the lesson surfaces are still fluid and land at less than half that.

On the specific checks the brief asks for: the **Fist-to-Five is present and correctly placed** in both new-learning lessons - step 9 in L2-D1 (before the two readiness questions) and step 12 in L5-D1 (before the exit ticket). **Timing holds**: 48 and 50 minutes against a 50-minute day, though Control's own budget badge says 55 (**B9**) and will let a teacher overrun. **Transitions are not authored as their own records** in either lesson - no `transition-hustle` or `transition-reset` appears in the 12- or 15-step lineups, so physical moves are absorbed into neighbouring instruction and unaccounted for. **Learning check to routes**: L2-D1's two readiness items are a `structured-numeric` factor-pair build and a multiple-choice - a defensible pairing, because the structured item separates a student who cannot find pairs from one who can but miscomputes, which multiple choice cannot. The routing itself is invisible to students and visible to the teacher only on the Remote's visit list, which is correct. What happens to a one-right-one-wrong student I could not observe, because no student on this roster can write.

**Confusion framing:** it is in the product, but as content rather than as behaviour. `C.C.1 Culture Day 1: Confusion Is Step One` is a published lesson. I found no place in the running software where an *attempt* is rewarded rather than a correct answer - the signal chips ("I'm stuck", "Say that again", "I've got this") are the closest thing, and they are a channel to the teacher, not a reward. That is the gap worth closing: a structured-numeric box that says "you found four of the six pairs, keep going" is a very different message from a red X, and the `pairs()` scoring already distinguishes *invented* from *missing* pairs, so the information to say it exists.

**Attribution check: passes.** No file attributes the confusion-is-step-one framing to Carnegie. Three separate places explicitly forbid it, in almost identical language, and `grep -rin carnegie src/` returns nothing at all. This one is clean.

## 12. The product as a whole

**What this does that no commercial curriculum platform does.** It runs the *room*, not the content. Every publisher ships problem sets, adaptive practice and a teacher dashboard; none of them ships a timed classroom state machine with four differentiated surfaces and a behavioural precorrection label. The state strip is the sharpest idea in the product - naming eyes, voice, supplies and body *before* the transition that breaks them, in a fixed slot order students read by position, with an all-or-nothing rule so a stale slot can never mislead. That is a real classroom-management insight encoded in software, and the fact that a partly-filled step renders nothing rather than something wrong is exactly the right call. The four-surface separation is the second one: main carries mathematics, pace carries directions, the Chromebook carries one action, the iPad carries everything students must not see. Watching a discussion step run - main showing the beat timeline, pace showing the stems, the student showing only the current beat - is genuinely impressive, and no platform I know of makes that distinction at all. Add the pen surface writing over the live slide on every display, and this is a coherent product with a real moat.

**What it is missing to be that.** Reliability discipline at the seams, and a closed evidence loop. The seams are where every serious defect in this report lives: a fix committed but not deployed; a projector that adopts a session while its twin does not; two clocks 38 seconds apart; a full-viewport overlay that covers a surface rather than sitting beside it; a tool name that resolves in one table and not the other. Every one is a *pairing* that drifted - two lists, two surfaces, two code paths - and the codebase already knows this, because CLAUDE.md documents the pattern repeatedly and the contract suite exists to catch it. The suite just does not cover these pairings yet. The evidence loop is the other half: the system is architecturally a data-collection product whose most important daily assessment writes to a table nothing reads.

**What I would cut.** The native slide-composition ambition, which the standing direction already shelves - correctly. Beyond that: delete `src/app/assignment/[id]/page.tsx:237` and `src/app/page.tsx:544-548`, the two full-roster alias pickers currently kept harmless only by an RLS grant that a documented rollback script would undo. Delete the three confirmed orphan components. Retire either the CRA state names or the I Do / We Do / You Do names from the bank - shipping both guarantees lessons that contradict themselves. And stop maintaining five creams; pick one, and let the contract suite fail on the sixth.

**Honest scope of this audit.** I ran one lesson, not four, because only six lessons are published and none of them is a practice day or a review day. I did not test a second student device, sleep/wake, offline recovery, join-late, double-click races, the back button, BRUH, Grudge Ball, `/teacher/studio` authoring, or assignment creation. I did not run `npm run typecheck` or `npm run build`. I could not exercise any student *write* path - signals, poll answers, tool evidence - because every one requires `requireVerifiedStudent`, and no student on this roster has an `auth_user_id` until the Workspace half of the FERPA cutover lands; a device that types a class code gets a provisional session and a 428 by design. That is the system behaving correctly, and it also means **the entire student write chain remains unproven end to end.** The first real verified student to tap a chip is still the test, and it should happen before the first class day, not during it.

**Three conflicts between the audit brief and CLAUDE.md**, resolved in CLAUDE.md's favour as instructed, and confirmed by Steele mid-audit: the lesson day is **50 minutes, not 55**; `/control`'s during-session view is **cream, not dark** (rule reversed 2026-07-29); and the misconception vocabulary is **13 tags**, not 18. The brief's estimate of ~440 emoji is also wrong - `src/` contains zero.
