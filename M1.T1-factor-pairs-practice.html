Divisibility rules — test 1 through 6
1Always. Every whole number is divisible by 1.
2Even. It ends in 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8.
3Digit sum. Add the digits — if that total is a multiple of 3, so is the number.
4Cut it in half. If the half is still even, the number is divisible by 4.
5Ends in 0 or 5.
6Both 2 and 3. It has to pass each of those rules.

Factor Pairs · Answer Key

Every pair, and the ordered list

6.NS.B.4
Success criterion: I can list all the factors of a number in sequential order. The list is complete when the two sides of the rainbow meet in the middle — the last pair is the one whose two factors are closest together.

Problems 1 and 2 are the graded target. Problem 3 is deliberately unmarked and is not required; it is worded and formatted exactly like the others so nobody decides in advance whether it is for them. Who reaches it tells you about persistence rather than ability, so use it to seed the next share-out and not to group anyone.
1
30 — 4 pairs meets at 5 × 6
Factor pairs 1×30 · 2×15 · 3×10 · 5×6
In order 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30 — 8 factors.
Watch for skipping 5×6 after 3×10, or writing 4× something (4 is not a factor — 30 ÷ 4 is not whole).
2
45 — 3 pairs meets between 5 and 9
Factor pairs 1×45 · 3×15 · 5×9
In order 1, 3, 5, 9, 15, 45 — 6 factors.
Watch for hunting for a 2 out of habit. 45 is odd, so the rules for 2 and 4 die on the first move — and that is the point of this number, so it is NOT hinted on the student page. A student running the method clears it in seconds; a student guessing stalls or invents 2×22.5. Missing 5×9 is the common slip.
3
240 — 10 pairs meets at 15 × 16
Factor pairs 1×240 · 2×120 · 3×80 · 4×60 · 5×48 · 6×40 · 8×30 · 10×24 · 12×20 · 15×16
In order 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 16, 20, 24, 30, 40, 48, 60, 80, 120, 240 — 20 factors.
Watch for stopping early, or writing 7, 9, 11, 13, 14 — none divide 240. The last pair, 15×16, is the one students most often miss.
Look for a margin note. The rules for 1 through 6 deliver six of the ten pairs and then run out, because 240 does not close until 15. The rest needs structure: 240 is 24 with a zero on it, so 24's whole factor list is inside it. Anyone who writes same as 24, an arrow, or x10 found that DURING the work, which is the thing worth seeing. Expect two or three a period, not a third of the class.
